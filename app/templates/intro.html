Website Archive Submitter

An automated pipeline that discovers a website's public pages and permanently preserves them across web-archiving services — with a searchable repository of everything it finds and saves.

Search repository

What it does

Point it at one or more domains. It crawls, cleans up, and archives their public pages automatically — then remembers exactly what it found and what happened to it.

◎

Discovers

Finds a site's URLs via sitemap, robots.txt, internal links, canonical tags, feeds, and pagination.

⇄

Archives

Submits every new URL to the Wayback Machine and archive.today, without bypassing rate limits or CAPTCHAs.

▤

Remembers

Keeps a permanent record of when a page was found, when it was archived, and a link to every snapshot.

↻

Resumes

Survives crashes and restarts — an interrupted submission queue always picks back up where it left off.

How it works

Six stages, running for one domain or many at once, on a persistent queue rather than a fire-and-forget script.

1

Add a domain

You give it a URL; it registers a new tracked project.

2

Crawl & discover

Sitemaps, robots.txt, and a same-domain link crawl surface every reachable page.

3

Normalize & dedupe

URLs are cleaned up and compared against what's already known, so only genuinely new pages move forward.

4

Queue for submission

New and never-archived URLs are placed on a persistent, resumable queue.

5

Submit to archives

A background worker sends each URL to the Wayback Machine and archive.today, retrying failures with backoff.

6

Track in the dashboard

Every domain's progress, successes, failures, and snapshot links are visible and searchable.
